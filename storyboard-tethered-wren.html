Tethered storyboard · Wren & the Kite, told by the Kite

Logline: A red kite envies the wild swifts and fights the string that holds it. When the string snaps it gets its freedom — and learns why kites need a string — while Wren, the ten-year-old who never lets go first, waits, searches, and mends. All narration is the Kite speaking, in first person. The Kite is the main character and the dynamic one; Wren is static.

Runtime ~7:30 · 18 plates + credits
Format 16:9, one narrator (the Kite), painted style

Moral / point “The string isn’t what holds you down. It’s what holds you up.” Same moral as Tether, retold from inside the kite: the pull we resent is the other half of our shape. Wren does not change — she holds, waits, searches, mends, holds again. The Kite does.

Story arc (Kite’s altitude = emotion)

Characters (locked across all 12 plates)

  • Kite (main, dynamic, narrator): red diamond, no face, long tail ribbon with small bows. The ribbon is the face: curls with want, whips angry, hangs limp when hopeless, streams straight at peace. Carries a yellow scarf patch + lumpy knot from beat 10 on.
  • Wren (static holder, age 10): dark brown ponytail, mid-blue coat, mustard-yellow scarf — shortened after she cuts a square from it to patch the kite. Never lets go first.
  • Swifts: wild and untethered — what the Kite wants to be.

Voice & holds — how the 5–10s rule works

Narrator: one voice, the Kite, first person, ~140 words/minute. Each plate below carries its exact passage of the story (all words are on this page). A plate stays on screen for its passage (~24–56s), but no single hold lasts longer than 10 seconds: each plate is covered in slow 7–9s push-in / reframe chapters with very, very slight motion, per the animation direction — the paintings are the star, stillness does the work.

Act I · The Pull
BEAT 10:00 – 0:24 · ~55 words · 3× 8s holds

1. First memory the bond

I woke in Wren’s hands.

That is my first memory: grass smell, the warmth of her fingers around my sticks, careful the way she holds everything she loves. She carried me under her arm up the hill to the sea wind, her yellow scarf streaming out behind her like a second tail.

Holds
Wide dawn hill → slow push toward girl → hold on folded kite.
Sound
Wind, grass, soft morning. Narrator (the Kite) intimate, close-mic.
Ends on
The folded red kite, ribbon trailing, safe under her arm.
BEAT 20:24 – 0:58 · ~80 words · 4× 8.5s holds

2. She ran, I caught the bond

She ran. I caught. That is how it works.

The wind got under me and I jumped, and the string sang. Up through my spine I felt every step she took — her boots drumming, her sudden stop, her laugh. She let out more string and more, and I climbed, and her scarf flew straight out, and for a while we were one line: girl, string, me.

For a while that was enough.

Holds
Follow the string girl→kite → kite soaring → wide one-line shot.
Sound
The Tug motif whole: low + high together. Joyful.
Ends on
One unbroken line: girl, string, kite.
BEAT 30:58 – 1:39 · ~95 words · 5× 8s holds

3. The end of the string the want

Then I hit the end of the string.

It stopped me with a jerk that went through my sticks. And just past me, where I could not go, the swifts came pouring over the cliff.

They did not flap the way gulls flap. They cut. They poured and swirled and dove out over the open sea, screaming thin screams, and never once looked down at a string. My tail curled out toward them before I told it to. It wanted to follow.

I leaned. The string said no.

Holds
Kite POV as swifts sweep past → tail curling → tiny girl below.
Sound
Swift-screams, woodwind swirl; motif cut off by the string going taut.
Ends on
Tail ribbon curled toward the horizon.
BEAT 41:39 – 2:35 · ~130 words · 7× 8s holds

4. Fighting the string rising tension

Anyone who has never been tied will tell you the tie is simple. It is not. It hums. It pulls at the center of you and will not let you forget it, even when the wind is good.

So I fought it.

I dove left. I swung right. I yanked. Below me Wren thought it was a game. She laughed and dug in her heels, skidding a little in the grass, delighted. That made me yank harder. I hated her a little then, for laughing.

The string sawed back and forth over the old fence post on the cliff. I felt each pass as a shiver. I did not know — I know now — that with every pass one more fiber let go. Wren did not see it either. She was watching me, not the post.

Holds
Kite thrashing wide → post extreme close-up, fraying → faster cutting.
Sound
Motif faster, off-balance; each scrape a rasp.
Ends on
One last strand, still taut.

Act II · Loose

5. Snap turning point

A big gust came off the sea.

Ping.

The pull vanished. I shot up like a cork out of water. For one second I saw her flat on her back in the grass, hands empty, mouth open, the limp end of the string falling beside her. She pushed up on one elbow and reached after me.

I did not stay to watch. I was free.

Holds
White flash → string bursts apart at the post (hold through the spoken Ping) → shocked Wren flat, kite flung up → cut to her on her back, crying, reaching after it.
Sound
Ping, two beats of silence, then a huge upbeat swell.
Ends on
Her reaching hand; the kite a high speck.

6. Free false victory

Oh, the climb. I broke through the cloud into gold. The sea below went copper. The air was smooth and huge and mine. The swifts took me in. They ringed me and screamed and I looped with them, rolled with them, dove with them. My broken end trailed under me, swaying, useless and light. I thought: this is what I am. Not a toy. This.

Holds

Sound

Ends on

BEAT 7a3:24 – 4:00 · tumbling, spinning down
BEAT 7b4:00 – 4:14 · branches rushing up

7. Nothing to push against the fall

Then they peeled away toward the horizon.

They went faster than I can go. I tried to follow and the wind shifted, just a small shift, the kind Wren’s hands used to answer without thinking.

A tethered kite leans against its string and climbs. I had nothing to lean against.

I flipped over.

I righted myself. I flipped again. I spun down through the cloud into purple and then into dark, spinning faster, the air slapping me first on one face then the other. There was no one holding the other end of me. I understood, falling, that the string had never been a leash. It had been the other half of my shape.

The thorn tree caught me.

Holds
Stall and wobble → spinning fall, swaying → thorn branches rushing up to meet it.
Sound
Flute tries its high note, keeps getting cut off; wind louder.
Ends on
A rough crunch in branches.
BEAT 8a4:14 – 4:42 · tangled, rain, sobbing
BEAT 8b4:42 – 4:46 · the warm dot of light

8. The thorn tree all is lost

Rain came in the night. It was the only thing that moved. I hung torn, my tail knotted in the branches, my red face dark with wet. The sky was empty. The swifts were gone. Wren was gone. For the first time I felt how small I was — a wet rag on thorns, nothing to push against, nothing to pull.

Then, on the hill below, a warm dot of light.

Holds
Still rain on the wreck — her voice breaks into sobs → cut past the branches: the lantern climbing.
Sound
Near-silence (rain, her sobs), then low D2 + piano under the light.
Ends on
Warm light on the hill below.
Act III · Taut
BEAT 9a4:46 – 5:06 · lantern walk to the tree
BEAT 9b5:06 – 5:20 · the catch

9. The search reconnection

She came slowly. Even from far off I knew her walk in the swinging light — quick, stopping, the yellow scarf dark with rain. She carried a lantern that swung with each step. It took her a long time. She did not run. When she reached the tree she stood right under me and stretched up on her toes, fingers reaching.

She missed. She tried again. A thorn scratched the back of her hand and she gasped and kept reaching.

Holds
She crosses to the tree, lantern high → close on her scratched hands; the kite drops toward them.
Sound
Solo piano picks up the low note, quietly, waiting.
Ends on
Cupped hands; the kite an instant from landing.
BEAT 10a5:20 – 5:31 · caught, carried home
BEAT 10b5:31 – 5:52 · the mend

10. The mend reconnection

I dropped into her hands.

She carried me home under her coat to keep me dry. At the kitchen table, in lamplight, she worked. She cleaned the tear. She cut a square from her own yellow scarf — the streaming one, the beautiful one — and sewed it over my wound, and she tied my broken string back together. The knot is lumpy and obvious. She did not hide it. She tugged it once to test it, and it held.

Holds
Caught at last → carried home under her coat through the rain → close-ups of hands: scarf square, needle, knot pulled tight.
Sound
Piano low note holds; the high note still waited for.
Ends on
The lumpy knot, glinting.

BEAT 115:52 – 6:28 · climb, then the wide arrives early

11. Leaning in climax

Morning on the hill. The same wind.

She ran. I caught. The wind hit me and the old anger rose up — pull, fight, prove — and then I felt the string go taut, felt her weight at the end of it through the knot, steady, waiting, never having let go first.

So I leaned into it instead of fighting it.

The pull sent me up. Higher than the fence post. Higher than the cloud gold. The swifts came back and circled me, screaming, and left again for the open sea. I watched them go. My tail streamed straight and steady behind me.

Holds
Track along the taut string past the glinting knot → kite climbing past old heights → dissolve early to the final wide under her last lines.
Sound
The Tug complete at last: low + high together, full strings.
Ends on
Tail streaming straight and steady; her scarf visibly shorter.
